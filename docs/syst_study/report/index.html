What drives the detector systematics

SBND Run 1 and ICARUS Run 2/4 detector variations, medulla v1.1.0 / PROfit v3.0.4. Overnight study, 2026-10-10. No cvwgt anywhere. Nothing committed.

Summary

The question was what drives each detector systematic and whether the shipped ratio histograms can be trusted. The study rebuilt every ratio from event-level paired tables (nominal and varied sample, same generated events) and attacked the four questions you listed. Confidence tags: established tested directly and reproducible; supported consistent with several independent checks but partly inferential; open not settled by available data; defect something that should change.

establishedThe ratios are small imbalances of large flows. All 20 production histograms are reproduced exactly (max |difference| 0.0) from the paired tables. Every variation selects 2–13% of events in only one of the two samples, in each direction. The net ratio is 1–12% of that churn. 95–99.6% of the flips are changes of the production selection chain (the event is in only one selected tree); only DENT has a sizeable post-cut share (11%).

establishedEnergy shifts are not the mechanism. reco_visible_energy is built from track length (CSDA range for contained tracks, MCS otherwise; medulla particle_variables.h:234-250, variables.h:127-140). Under charge-type variations 67–78% of events keep a bit-identical energy. The energy dependence of the ratios comes from selection flips at the species decision boundaries (muon with its 2× boost, leading proton, 50 MeV proton threshold, plus sub-leading particles we cannot see).

supportedQ1: why ratios trend with energy. The sample composition changes with energy. In SBND the 1.5–2.0 GeV bin is 92% multi-proton (mean multiplicity 3.3) because contained muons carry only 45% of the energy there; ICARUS saturates at 39% multi-proton. The flip probability grows with proton multiplicity (SBND ChargeScale15: 5.7% for 1p to 11.8% for 4+p). A cross-validated model using only PID scores and multiplicity, with no energy input, reproduces the energy dependence of the net effect up to 1.5 GeV (within about 0.5–1%). It explains only about half of the deficit in the last bin, and that last bin holds about 600 events. The 1p and multi-proton channels respond very differently, yet one inclusive histogram is applied to both.

establishedQ2: yes, noise is being assessed as systematic. The paired bootstrap is well calibrated (split-half χ2/ndf 1.00; block tests 1.00–1.01), but pairing removes only independent-sample error (1.9–5.2×); the churn noise remains. Of 20 production ratios, 11 are smooth trends, 5 are consistent with a constant offset and 4 with zero. Noise is 40–43% of the raw covariance trace for ICARUS and 21% for the SBND set in systematics_suite.xml. A BIC-selected smooth fit beats the raw bins on held-out data for all 20 pairs.

establishedQ3: the direct DetVar method can reproduce the ratios. With an intermediate tree that carries every pool event on both sides and a selected weight, native PROfit reproduces the production ratio to 1e-7 per bin (one-line patch to keep zero weights, or a 1e-9 “epsilon” weight with unmodified PROfit). Today’s matched mode cannot: it never stores zero-weight events, so CV-only and variation-only events vanish (deviations up to 17%).

supportedQ4: charge scale. SBND ChargeScale changes the calorimetric KE by −1.5% (muons) and −2.3% (protons); ICARUS by +0.6% (muons) and −1.2% (protons), opposite in sign for muons. Its large SBND tilt (−7%/GeV, −13% in the last bin) is a multi-proton effect (the 1p ratio is flat); ICARUS is flat (−1%, slope 0). Event-weighted, the two are closer than the headline suggests (rms 1.7% vs 1.0–1.2%; integrated −0.65% vs −1.0%). The PROfit “remove one systematic” scans cannot resolve SBND vs ICARUS because every variant, including trigger, sits at the same χ2 ≈ 1.16× the baseline. My Asimov toy (illustrative only) gives similar SBND and ICARUS effects only if the cross-detector normalization is tight (<0.3%).

defectICARUS Run 4 WireModxThetaXW contains duplicated events in the varied sample (13,878 duplicated keys in its events tree; about 1,100 in the selected tree). The shipped ratio is biased high by +0.5 to +1.1% in every bin. Run 2 and the de-duplicated Run 4 agree (+0.49% vs +0.52%).

defectOther issues needing a decision: the single ratio is applied to both proton channels (SBND ChargeScale15 1p deviates by up to 17%, paired χ2/ndf 187/17); ICARUS ratios omit the filterEE cut the analysis applies (mostly within noise, significant for Run 2 WireModxThetaXW); truth is missing in the SBND WireMod varied trees; the varied samples cover only 74–99% of the nominal events (flat in energy); the analysis deliberately uses the same 15% smearing at both detectors although SBND fitted 5.1% (it raises the SBND effect by about 28%, section 6).

Top actions: (1) regenerate ICARUS Run 4 WireModxThetaXW after de-duplication; (2) smooth the ratios (section 4) and treat zero-class variations as zero or constant; (3) decide on channel-specific ratios (native DetVar makes this cheap); (4) apply filterEE to the ICARUS calculator selection; (5) rerun the “no X” sensitivity scans from one consistent configuration; (6) keep the deliberate 15% smearing at both detectors; optionally add a zero-smearing sample.

1 Method and validation

Inputs are the v1.1.0 detvar files and the production ratio histograms detvar/detsys-v1.1.0v2.root. For each of 20 detector/variation pairs (SBND: ChargeScale, ChargeScale15, SCE0x/2x, WireModxThetaXW, WireModYZ, MattModYZ, Gain high/low, DENT; ICARUS Run 2 and Run 4: ChargeScale, SCE0x/2x, WireModxThetaXW, WireModYZ) I rebuilt the pool the way detsys/calculator does: events processed in both samples (key Run, Subrun, Evt, event_max_neutrino_energy rounded to 1e−6), selected rows restricted to the pool, the calculator’s cut applied (SBND: fiducial, veto, crosser == 0, |xend| ≥ 4 cm; ICARUS: fiducial and veto). Each row carries a selected flag and the candidate’s features on each side.

establishedValidation. Counting rows reproduces all 20 production histograms with max |diff| = 0.0. Dropping rows with repeated keys (needed to pair events one-to-one) moves the ratio by <1e−3, except ICARUS Run 4 WireModxThetaXW (1.3e−2, see section 8). SBND has 0.6% repeated keys, ICARUS about 0.1% (most likely several interactions sharing an event; they appear in the nominal and varied samples alike).

What the selection does

The medulla selection is cut based: fiducial; no photons, electrons, charged pions above 25 MeV; exactly one muon above 143.4 MeV; at least one proton above 50 MeV. Species come from SPINE softmax scores with a 2× muon boost. I verified the rule on the stored scores: 100% of selected muon candidates satisfy 2sμ > max(sπ, sp) (only 95.0% would satisfy the unboosted rule in SBND, 98.4% in ICARUS), and 100% of leading protons satisfy sp > max(2sμ, sπ). I use these two margins throughout.

Pool coverage

The varied samples cover only part of the nominal events: 81–99% for SBND (WireModxThetaXW 81%), 74–99% for ICARUS. established The covered fraction is flat in energy (within ±1%) for all 19 pairs, so the pool does not bias energy dependence. (DENT uses its own CV sample.)

Where the flips happen

supported Lost events sit at the leading-proton margin (standardized mean difference −0.55 to −0.83 versus kept events) and the muon margin (−0.40 to −1.05), plus the 50 MeV proton threshold (the 50–60 MeV proton bin loses 14–38% against a floor of 5–11%). Gained events show the same pattern on the varied side. A gradient-boosted classifier with PID scores only predicts “lost” with AUC 0.73–0.88 (energy alone: 0.53–0.57); the weakest PID predictability is for SCE and DENT (0.73–0.78), the strongest for ChargeScale and Gain (0.82–0.88).

A small population of fragile events

supported Whether an event is dropped is far from independent across variations. In SBND, 9.1% of nominal-selected events are dropped by at least three of nine variations, against 2.1% expected for independent draws at the same mean loss rate; 0.07% are dropped by all nine. The ICARUS runs show the same effect (3.3–3.5% vs 0.2% for ≥3 of 5). These events carry 38–61% of all loss incidents. They are background-enriched (signal share 0.60–0.65 against 0.885 overall) and sit at the species boundaries. A floor of 3–9% lost events exists even for confident events; I cannot attribute it, because the failing side stores no features.

openPhysics or chaos? The data cannot tell whether the 2–13% flows are a real detector response or the network’s chaotic reaction to any perturbation of the voxels. The odd/even structure of SCE (net odd, churn even) says the churn is mostly the latter while the net carries the physics. A same-seed rerun of the nominal, or a variation with the identity transform, would measure the churn floor directly. It does not exist.

3 Q1: why do some systematics trend with energy?

Only SBND shows significant energy trends (Table 1): ChargeScale and ChargeScale15 fall (−5.8 and −7.2 %/GeV), WireModYZ (not used) and MattModYZ rise (+7.7, +1.8), Gain rises or falls with its sign (+16 / −7), DENT is marginal (+2.5 ± 0.9). Every ICARUS slope is within 2.6σ of zero (at most +1.3 ± 0.5 %/GeV). So the question has two halves: why SBND trends, and why ICARUS does not.

3.1 It is not an energy shift established

If the variations shifted the energy scale, a steeply falling spectrum would turn a constant fractional shift δ into a ratio trend 1 + (α−1)δ. That is not what happens. Track KE is a function of reconstructed length: within a 20 cm length bin the muon KE has an rms of 12 MeV, exactly the spread the bin width implies, and medulla computes it by CSDA range for contained tracks (particle_variables.h:234-250). The calorimetric energy enters only for showers, and the selection contains none. For ChargeScale15, 67% of events have a bit-identical Evis and 81% an identical muon length; the median fractional shift is 0.00%. The only energy migration left is overflow across the 2.0 GeV edge (−1.5% of the last bin for ChargeScale15, −3.4% for Gain −5%).

3.2 The sample composition changes with energy supported

In SBND the multi-proton fraction rises from 3% (0.3–0.5 GeV) to 92% (1.5–2.0 GeV); the mean multiplicity reaches 3.3. In ICARUS the multi-proton fraction saturates at 39–42% and the mean multiplicity at 1.6.

3.3 The flip hazard grows with multiplicity supported

For SBND ChargeScale15 the loss probability is 5.7, 7.5, 8.6, 11.8% for 1, 2, 3, 4–7 protons; for ICARUS Run 2 ChargeScale 4.1, 4.5, 4.9, 7.1%. Each additional particle is another chance to cross a species boundary or a threshold, and any crossing that produces a pion-like, electron-like or photon-like fragment vetoes the event. This is the natural reading, but I could not observe sub-leading particles directly (see 3.5).

3.4 Which flipped population carries the trend supported

For SBND ChargeScale15 the net in six coarse bins is +1.05, −0.02, −1.34, −2.72, −4.13, −11.7%. The gain from muon-marginal candidates (short muons close to the 2× boost boundary) falls from 3.05% to about 0.5% with energy, while the proton-marginal loss stays at 2–3% and rises to 5.8% in the last bin. Losses where neither leading candidate is marginal grow from 3% to 10% at the highest energy. ICARUS has the same pieces (proton-marginal loss 3.3% to 1.4%, muon-marginal gain 0.8% to 0.05%) but they fall in step, so the net stays flat near −1%.

3.5 A model without energy explains most of the trend supported

A cross-validated gradient-boosted classifier using only PID scores, calorimetric differences and proton multiplicity (no energy, no kinematics) predicts the lost and gained fractions per energy bin. The predicted net effect:

3.6 The last bin is small and special established

The 1.5–2.0 GeV bin of SBND has about 600 selected events (ICARUS: 2,700–5,500). A churn of 20–30% means 100–200 flips, so the statistical error on the ratio is 2–3%; the 13% deficit of ChargeScale15 is still a 6σ effect. It contains high-multiplicity events (mean 3.2–4.0 protons; lost and gained events have a lower muon score, 0.86–0.89 against 0.94 for kept events). The Q2 study finds that ChargeScale, ChargeScale15 and GainLow miss the extrapolation of the smooth fit through the lower bins by −2.1, −2.7, −3.0σ there, which looks like overflow migration out of the 2.0 GeV edge rather than a smooth continuation.

supportedAnswer to Q1. A systematic trends with energy when (a) it changes the PID scores near species boundaries and (b) the sample’s susceptibility to boundary crossings itself changes with energy, which in SBND is driven by the rising multiplicity and the falling muon share. The sign depends on the sign of the asymmetry between the lost and gained flows: ChargeScale loses more than it gains at high energy (down), Gain +5% and MattModYZ/WireModYZ gain more (up). ICARUS has no trend because its composition and hazard do not change with energy.

4 Q2: is statistical noise being assessed as systematics?

Using identical events in the CV and varied samples removes the independent-sample error: a naive independent-Poisson error would be 1.9–5.2× larger (median 2.9×). What remains is the churn: each variation flips 2–13% of events, those flips are random draws from the network’s point of view, and the net effect carries their sampling noise. The question is how much. (Full report: Q2_statistical_content.md.)

4.1 The errors are right established

The paired Poisson bootstrap agrees with the delta-method variance of the cross-classified table (ratio 1.002, range 0.991–1.008; both are the same first-order quantity, so this checks the implementation). The independent tests: random event splits give χ2/ndf 1.00 (0.98–1.03, 400 splits), splits by Run or (Run, Subrun) 1.007, a delete-one-block jackknife 1.005 and a block bootstrap 1.000. No inflation factor is needed. Typical per-bin errors: SBND 0.41% (last bin 2.9%), ICARUS 0.5–0.6%.

4.2 Signal versus noise established

Residuals about fixed-degree smooth fits have pooled χ2/ndf 0.98–1.03 and uniform p-values; bulk-bin pulls have sd 0.93. So the bin-to-bin scatter is statistical. Classification of the 20 production ratios: 11 smooth trends, 5 constant offsets (SBND SCE2x, ICARUS Run 2 ChargeScale and SCE2x, Run 4 ChargeScale and SCE2x) and 4 consistent with zero (SBND WireModxThetaXW, ICARUS Run 2 and Run 4 WireModYZ, ICARUS Run 4 WireModxThetaXW before de-duplication). A pure-noise ratio still has E|r−1| = 0.8σ per bin; the large variations (Gain, ChargeScale, WireModYZ) sit 2–15× above that floor, the zero class sits at it. On a held-out half, a smooth fit predicts better than the raw bins for all 20 pairs, removing about 88% of the removable noise.

4.3 What PROfit sees established

PROfit builds the fractional covariance from (r−1)(r−1)T, so noise enters as systematic signal. For ICARUS the noise is 43% (Run 2) and 40% (Run 4) of the raw trace and 1.7–2.0× the dominant ChargeScale trace; smoothing lowers the effective rank from 2.7 to 1.5 (Run 2) and 2.1 to 1.4 (Run 4) and raises the adjacent-bin correlation from 0.43 to 0.99 (0.65 to 0.99 for Run 4). In SBND the noise is 6.8% of the trace summed over all ten variations but 21% for the set in the XML; 56–65% of each variation’s noise trace sits in the last bin. Noise-dominated variations (noise/raw ≥ 0.5): SBND WireModxThetaXW and DENT, ICARUS Run 2 SCE2x, WireModxThetaXW, WireModYZ, Run 4 SCE2x and WireModYZ.

4.4 Consistency tests as noise probes supported

  • ICARUS Run 2 against Run 4 (independent samples): pooled χ2/ndf 105/85 (p = 0.07). Consistent with noise; one real run-dependent effect is not excluded.
  • SBND ChargeScale is proportional to ChargeScale15 (factor 0.78, χ2/16 = 1.32). The two are the same effect at different strength.
  • SCE0x and SCE2x are antisymmetric within noise in ICARUS, marginally in SBND (p = 0.012); the even part is noise. See section 7.
  • WireModYZ against MattModYZ is only roughly proportional (factor about 6, p = 0.004); Gain high and low show a real nonlinear response.
  • Noise is positively correlated between SBND variations (median 0.26) because they share the nominal sample.

supportedRecommendation from Q2. Smooth every variation with a GLS Legendre polynomial in ln E on bins 1–16 (degree chosen by BIC among zero and 0–4). Keep the last bin raw if its pull against the bulk extrapolation exceeds 2σ, otherwise shrink it towards the extrapolation (edge variance 0.95%); keeping it raw is the conservative option. Do not smooth blindly through the last bin: ChargeScale, ChargeScale15 and GainLow miss the extrapolation by 2.1–3.0σ. Treat the noise-dominated variations as a constant or zero. In the closure study the mean squared error relative to the raw ratio is 0.34–0.52 for smooth truths and 0.15–0.35 for zero or constant truths. Caveat: a polynomial in ln E is an assumption (the Whittaker smoother gives similar results), and smoothing assumes the physical response is smooth in Evis, which section 3.4 says is plausible but not proven for features near the 0.9–1.2 GeV bins.

Checks that came out null: restricting to the common pool of all variations leaves ratios unchanged (χ2 320 for 323 degrees of freedom summed over 19 pairs); the 0.6% repeated keys move the ratio by a median 0.08σ. Not analysed: Gain (commented out in the XML), the combined SCE_icarus histogram, ProtonMiss.

5 Q3: can the direct DetVar method reproduce the systematics?

established Yes, to float precision, with the construction you proposed. The work was done on a patched copy of PROfit (/Users/mueller/Projects/profit-asymmetric-q3); your checkout is untouched. Full report: Q3_direct_detvar.md.

5.1 What PROfit does

  • CV and variation trees go through separate propellers; every event gets mc_weight = product(branch weights, additional_weight) × det_pot/mc_pot, and if(mc_weight==0) return; (PROcreate.cxx:1374) means zero-weight events are never stored.
  • With cv_variation_matching_vars, keys are the int-rounded values of the matching variables; the common set is keys present in the CV and all variations. Together with the line above, only events selected on both sides survive (matched mode).
  • The response is the per-bin ratio of the variation to the CV spectra in the observable’s own binning, inserted into the same spline machinery as hist1d. No statistical uncertainty of the response is propagated by either method (the production histograms carry zero errors).

5.3 Assessment

Gains: any binning without rerunning the calculator; the response follows the analysis’s own selection and channels (for reco_proton_multiplicity == 1 the response differs from the inclusive ratio by up to 17% for ChargeScale15; numpy only, section 8); one consistent event list across observables. Losses and risks: per-subchannel responses are noisier, and no statistical error is propagated, so noise would be treated as systematic signal unless smoothing is added; matched mode forces an intersection pool; trees must carry every branch the selection uses; zero-denominator bins behave differently from the calculator (ratio set to 1 there). Pitfalls found: the prototype key event_max_neutrino_energy is cast to integer GeV, so it is not a key (ICARUS Run 2 has 400 colliding (Run, Subrun, Evt)); use an integer Ei = round(1e6 E). Intermediate trees written by assigning a dict in uproot become RNTuples that segfault PROfit; use mktree. Not tested natively: a full analysis XML with per-subchannel truth cuts, multi-section DetVar combination, the flash-PE cuts. Recommendation: keep the ratio histograms as the reference, validate any native configuration against them first, and adopt native DetVar with the selected-attribute construction where binning flexibility or exclusive channels matter (DENT, end-x, proton channels).

6 Q4: the charge-scale systematic

Definition: a nonlinear multiplicative scale of every deposition’s charge (third-order polynomial f(q)) plus a random Gaussian smearing of each deposition; the coefficients and the smearing are fitted separately per detector to the calo-versus-range energy bias (technote: s = 0.0511 for SBND, 0.149 for ICARUS). SBND ChargeScale is the nonlinear scale with about 5% smearing; ChargeScale15 (tree sbnd_detvar_smear15) is the same with the smearing turned up to 15% (definition confirmed by the analysis team after the first version of this report; the first version listed it as unknown). The analysis uses SBND ChargeScale15 and ICARUS ChargeScale, whose fitted smearing is 15%, so the SBND 15% setting puts both detectors at the same smearing.

supportedConsequence for the SBND systematic. The data-fitted SBND smearing is 5.1%, so ChargeScale15 triples it. Comparing the two (same events, same scale function) isolates the smearing: the mean calorimetric shift is the same to 0.05 points, but the effect on the selection is larger with 15%: integrated net −0.65% against −0.46%, last bin −13.2% against −10.3%, event-weighted rms 1.70% against 1.37%, slope −7.2 against −5.8 %/GeV, churn 11.9% against 10.5%. Extrapolating the two points to zero smearing (quadratic in s) the pure nonlinear scale carries about 67–79% of the ChargeScale15 effect (56–71% if linear in s), so the 15% smearing adds roughly 25–40%. The smear-induced part of the per-event calorimetric spread is small (muon 0.13% at 5%, 0.39% at 15%; proton 0.29% and 0.85%) against a smearing-independent spread of 0.43% (muon) and 1.07% (proton). Two points cannot prove the functional form; a zero-smearing sample would. The 15% is deliberate: the same level of smearing is used at both detectors even though SBND fitted lower. It is a conservative harmonization, and it makes the SBND effect about 28% larger than the fitted 5.1% (ChargeScale) would; that cost is known and intended.

6.1 What it does to the reconstruction established

  • SBND ChargeScale15: muon calorimetric KE −1.9% at 150 MeV to −1.3% at 1 GeV; proton −3.0% at 60 MeV to −1.7% above 450 MeV.
  • ICARUS ChargeScale: muon +0.2% to +0.7%, proton −1.7% to −0.2%. The sign for muons is opposite to SBND.
  • SBND Gain −5%: −4.4% for muons at all energies and −2.1% to −3.8% rising for protons, i.e. a different shape: a linear gain change moves all charge equally while the fitted charge scale is nonlinear and suppresses large depositions.

The calorimetric shift is small (about −1.5%) compared with the 6% churn it causes. Median PID score shifts are tiny too (muon softmax +0.03 at 143–300 MeV, proton softmax −0.007 at 50–100 MeV); the effect lives in the tails near species boundaries, which is where lost and gained events are found (section 2).

6.2 Why SBND tilts: a multi-proton effect supported

The deviation of the 1p channel from the combined ratio is significant: paired-bootstrap χ2/ndf 187/17 for ChargeScale15 and 233/17 for ChargeScale (largest single-bin deviation 4.2σ and 5.2σ); for ICARUS ChargeScale it is 43–51/17 (2.5σ to 3.6σ). Together with the composition (section 3.2), this is the mechanism: the SBND tilt is high-multiplicity events being lost. Two further SBND-specific facts: the loss probability rises with proton KE above 300 MeV (4.7% at 150–200 MeV to 8.1% above 500 MeV) whereas it keeps falling in ICARUS (3.4% to 2.3%), and at fixed multiplicity SBND flips are 1.3–1.6× more likely than ICARUS (loss for 1, 2, 3, 4+ protons: 5.7, 7.5, 8.6, 11.8% against 4.1, 4.5, 4.9, 7.1%).

6.4 Why the sensitivity impact looks similar open

What the PROfit scans say. From runs/legacy_root/contours_*_surf.root (30×30 grid, 90% contour at Δχ2 = 1.6): removing SBND ChargeScale gives a median χ2 ratio of 1.168, ICARUS ChargeScale 1.156. But every variant lies between 1.156 and 1.189, including trigger emulation (SBND 1.156, ICARUS 1.170), a systematic whose own effect is below 2% and in SBND below 0.01%. A uniform +16% for removing anything means the “full” run and the “no X” runs differ by something other than X (configuration, fit settings or cache). The scans therefore cannot rank individual systematics; they show only that the SBND and ICARUS charge-scale removals differ by 1% in χ2, which is below the resolution of the study. They also pre-date the v1.1.0v2 ratios and the end-point cut. defect They need rerunning from one configuration (jobs/contours/scan_detsyst.variants was built for that).

What my toy says (not PROfit; illustrative). Two-flavour νμ disappearance, reco-energy spectra of the nominal selected events at the “full” exposures, all active detector ratios as Gaussian nuisances (one-sided linear mirrored response, two-sided spline for SCE and trigger), a 10% shared flux/cross-section normalization and an uncorrelated normalization per detector/run, Asimov data without oscillation. Removing the SBND charge-scale nuisance improves the 90% limit by 9.3, 6.9, 5.3, 5.0% for an uncorrelated per-detector normalization of 0.3, 1, 3, 10%; removing the ICARUS one by 3.9, 1.4, 0.3, 0.4%. The shared flux uncertainty (5–20%) changes neither. Interpretation: the ICARUS charge scale is a nearly flat −1% shift, which is degenerate with a free relative normalization and matters only when the cross-detector normalization is known to better than about 1%. The SBND tilt is not degenerate with a normalization, but affects few events. So the two contribute similar χ2 when the normalization is tight (toy χ2 ratios 1.10 and 1.095 at 0.3%), and SBND dominates when it is loose. Whether the real analysis is in the tight regime depends on its flux/cross-section correlation structure and its POT/mass terms, which I did not reproduce. open The toy’s absolute event counts inherit the XML POT bookkeeping (the XML says SBND MC is 5.6e20 POT; the exposures in the comparisons are marked bogus), so only the relative comparison should be trusted.

supportedAnswer to Q4. The SBND charge scale is the largest used detector systematic by shape (rms 4%, −13% in the last bin) and by event-weighted rms (1.7%; Gain and WireModYZ are larger but not used). It is a selection effect: a nonlinear charge suppression plus smearing moves PID scores near species boundaries, and the sample’s multiplicity and muon-containment structure converts that into an energy tilt in SBND but a flat shift in ICARUS. Its apparent similar importance in the two detectors is a combination of comparable event-weighted effect sizes and the degeneracy of ICARUS’s flat shift with normalization; the PROfit scans cannot confirm it because of an unexplained common offset.

9 Self-critique: what could be wrong

  • Everything about mechanisms is correlational. Only selected events are stored, so for a lost event I see its nominal features but never the varied-side state that made it fail. I identify where flips sit (margins, thresholds, multiplicity), not why a particular event fails. 45–70% of the loss sits at a floor of 3–9% among events far from any boundary, and I cannot attribute it. The sub-leading particles that probably carry it are not stored. The statement “sub-leading particles explain the last-bin excess” is a hypothesis.
  • The margins are approximations of the real argmax (electron and photon scores are ignored; the three-class softmax closes to 1 for both candidates, and 100% of selected events pass the boosted rule, which supports but does not prove the definition).
  • The classifier closure is in-sample across energy. The model is cross-validated by events but trained on a sample dominated by low energies; its last-bin underprediction mixes real missing information with too few high-multiplicity training events.
  • The noise model treats the flips as random draws. If part of the churn is a deterministic response to the specific perturbation, the bootstrap would under- or overstate the noise; a same-seed rerun is the only test. The Q2 calibration (split-half and block tests at 1.00) validates event sampling, not seed reproducibility.
  • Smoothing assumes smoothness in Evis. It is supported by the pulls (sd 0.93) but not proven for localized features, and the last bin is a documented exception.
  • “Event-weighted rms” is not sensitivity. It is an effect size; the toy is a toy. Absolute counts in the toy inherit possibly unreliable POT bookkeeping; the PROfit scans are internally inconsistent.
  • What I verified myself from raw files and source: exact reproduction of the 20 histograms; the Evis definition (source and data); the boosted-rule check; flips by tier; hazard against multiplicity; composition against energy; channel and filterEE audits with paired bootstraps; the Run 4 duplicates (events tree and selected tree, de-duplicated ratio); the pool coverage; the PROfit scan offset; the toy. What I took from the subagent reports after reading them but not re-deriving: the PROfit native construction (Q3), the bootstrap calibration and smoothing closure (Q2), and the odd/even SCE, WireModYZ/MattMod, DENT-halves and trigger results (per-systematic). Their tables are consistent with my numbers wherever the two overlap (e.g. +0.52% for the de-duplicated Run 4 ratio, trigger exactness).

10 Recommendations

  1. Regenerate ICARUS Run 4 WireModxThetaXW after de-duplicating the varied sample (and find why it was duplicated).
  2. Smooth the ratios (GLS polynomial in ln E, BIC degree, last bin raw if the pull exceeds 2σ) and treat the zero-class variations (SBND WireModxThetaXW, ICARUS WireModYZ and Run 4 xThetaXW) as zero or a constant; drop the SBND trigger.
  3. Resolve the channel mismatch: channel-resolved responses through native DetVar with the selected-attribute construction (validated to 1e−7), or at minimum separate 1p and Mp ratio histograms for SBND ChargeScale15 and ICARUS ChargeScale.
  4. Apply filterEE in the ICARUS calculator selection and fold the trigger and endx4 cuts into one configurable calculator.
  5. Rerun the “no X” sensitivity scans from one configuration with the v1.1.0v2 ratios; add the full-analysis check of whether the cross-detector normalization is in the tight regime.
  6. Measure the churn floor: an identity-transform reconstruction or a different-seed rerun of the nominal. It converts the main open question (physics versus chaos) into a number.
  7. Dump selection intermediates (pion, photon, electron multiplicity and KE, second muon) for events dropped in the varied sample; it settles the last-bin excess and the 3–9% floor.
  8. Keep the harmonized 15% smearing (deliberate; a zero-smearing sample would still separate the nonlinear scale from the smearing), decide the MattModYZ–ChargeScale overlap, and confirm the DENT map acts on the west TPC.
  9. Have the calculator write per-bin cross-classified counts (kept, lost, gained, migrating) so an analytic statistical error can ship with each ratio.
